01 — Overview

What Aegis ERP is

A finance & operations system for a fictional UAE company, Aegis FZE (currency AED, 5% VAT), rebuilt from an HTML prototype styled after Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central.

What works today:

  • General Ledger — chart of accounts, manual journal vouchers, the account ledger, and the trial balance.
  • Accounts Receivable — customers, sales invoices that automatically generate their GL entries, customer receipts with invoice allocation, AR aging, and customer statements.
  • Security — cookie-based login, three roles, and page-level authorization.
The defining property
Every financial document becomes a balanced, double-entry GL voucher, and the books are balanced by construction. You cannot post an invoice without its General Ledger entry, and you cannot post anything that doesn't balance.

04 — Primer

Accounting concepts you need first

The system rests on standard double-entry bookkeeping. Five ideas:

Chart of Accounts — the list of accounts money is tracked in. Each account has a Code (e.g. 12010), a name, and a type.

Normal balance — every account carries its balance on one side, derived from its type, never stored:

Account typeNormal balance
Asset, ExpenseDebit
Liability, Equity, IncomeCredit

Cost centers — a dimension tagging lines by segment (Admin, Passport Services, Travel & Tourism, HR).

Fiscal periods — accounting months. Every voucher posts into exactly one period, and its date must fall inside it.

Vouchers & double entry — the ledger unit is a JournalVoucher: a header plus two or more lines, each a debit or a credit. A voucher only posts when total debits = total credits.

Subledger over the GL — Accounts Receivable is a subledger: customers, invoices, and receipts live in their own tables, but the money always also flows through the GL control account 12010 – Accounts Receivable.

05 — Data

The data model

The core GL entities plus the AR subledger. Every posted document links to the JournalVoucher it generated.

Persistence rules: unique indexes on every document number; money is decimal(18,2); enums persist as readable strings; master records use restrictive deletes; the ASP.NET Identity tables share the same database.

06 — The engine

The posting engine — the heart of the system

Everything that touches the ledger goes through one place: JournalPoster. It builds and validates a GL voucher on the caller's DbContext and transaction, without committing — so a document and its GL entry commit together, atomically.

The validation rules

JournalVoucher.Post() is the gatekeeper. In order, it rejects:

#ConditionMessage
1Already postedVoucher is already posted.
2Was rejectedA rejected voucher cannot be posted.
3Fewer than 2 linesA voucher needs at least two lines.
4aA line has no accountLine N has no account selected.
4bA line is negativeLine N has a negative amount.
4cA line is both Dr and CrLine N cannot be both debit and credit.
4dA line has no amountLine N has no amount.
5Debits ≠ creditsVoucher is out of balance by X (Dr … / Cr …).

Document numbering

Numbers are PREFIX-YEAR-#### from the highest existing suffix + 1, so seeded or skipped numbers never collide. An invoice and its generated voucher share the same number.

TypePrefixExample
JournalJVJV-2026-0001
ReceiptRVRV-2026-0028
PaymentPVPV-2026-0038
Sales InvoiceINVINV-2026-0142
OpeningOBOB-2026-0001
Concurrency safety net
A unique-constraint clash (two users computing the same number at once) is translated into a friendly, recoverable error instead of a crash. This makes the losing writer fail cleanly; full race prevention is a hardening item.

08 — Runtime

Request lifecycle (Blazor Server)

The UI lives on the server and talks to the browser over a SignalR circuit. A click is a server event; the server re-renders and pushes a DOM diff back. Data access uses a DbContext factory — every service call spins up a short-lived context and disposes it, avoiding the shared-context bug.

09 — Module

General Ledger

Journal Voucher Posters

The manual double-entry screen — for entries with no source document (accruals, opening balances, corrections).

The other GL screens: Chart of Accounts (list + add), General Ledger (an account's opening balance, every posted line with a running balance, and closing — shown in the account's normal direction, Dr or Cr), and Trial Balance (all accounts split into Debit/Credit columns with a live “In balance” proof).

Subledger caveat
Customer subledger totals reflect posted AR documents only. The GL opening balance on account 12010 is not attributed to any customer, so the sum of customer outstandings can differ from the GL AR control balance until historical invoices are entered — expected for a fresh subledger, and noted in the UI.

13 — Reference

Reporting math

All reports derive from one projection: every journal line whose voucher is Posted, pulled into memory before any decimal arithmetic (so it runs identically on SQLite and PostgreSQL).

ReportFormula
Account ledgersign = +1 if debit-normal else −1; opening = sign × Σ(Dr − Cr) before the period; each row running += sign × (Dr − Cr)
Trial balanceper account net = Σ(Dr − Cr) to period end; positive → Debit column, negative → Credit column
Agingper open invoice: outstanding = Gross − Σ(allocated receipts), bucketed by (asOf − DueDate) in days
Statementinvoices as debits, receipts as credits, ordered by date, with a running balance

15 — Operate

Run & test

Run — dotnet run --project src/AegisErp.Web. First launch creates aegis_erp.db (SQLite) and seeds everything; sign in with a demo account.

Test — dotnet test. 17 xUnit tests cover posting rules, invoice → GL voucher construction and VAT, receipt allocation and over-allocation rejection, aging buckets, statements, and a full invoice + receipt cycle staying in balance.

Switch to PostgreSQL — set "Database": { "Provider": "Postgres" } and the connection string in appsettings.json. No code change.

16 — Honest notes

Known limitations & hardening

Deliberately deferred; none affect single-user SQLite dev, and the full fixes are PostgreSQL-specific.

  • Schema management — uses EnsureCreated(); switch to EF migrations before production (dotnet-ef is already pinned).
  • Concurrent document numbering — “max + 1” with no lock; the losing writer fails cleanly, but the proper fix is a per-(prefix, year) advisory lock or a Serializable transaction with retry.
  • Concurrent receipt allocation — the outstanding check isn't DB-enforced, so two simultaneous receipts could over-settle an invoice on Postgres. Fix: lock the invoice row or persist an allocated-total with a check constraint.
  • Period close — the engine refuses to post into a closed period, but there's no UI to close one yet.
17 — Ahead

Roadmap

The same Domain → Service → Blazor pattern extends to the rest:

  1. Accounts Payable — Vendors, Purchase Invoice, Payment Voucher (a mirror of AR).
  2. Credit / debit notes and multi-invoice receipt allocation.
  3. Approval workflows — the prototype's Initiator → Finance → CFO → Posted chains.
  4. Period close and the financial statements (P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow) off the ledger.
  5. Inventory, HR / Payroll (WPS), Fixed Assets with automated depreciation.
  6. Production hardening — migrations, concurrency locks, HTTPS/hosting, backups, a real user-management UI.
